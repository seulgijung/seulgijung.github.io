# Girl-slaughter: 37 Years of Sex-Selective Abortion in South Korea

An estimated 408,043 girls were never born in South Korea between 1981 and 2010 due to sex-selective abortion. This scrollytelling project traces the rise and fall of skewed sex ratios at birth — from the spread of ultrasound technology in the early 1980s to the Constitutional Court's 2024 ruling that struck down the fetal sex disclosure ban.

🔗 **[Read the story](https://seulgijung.github.io/aborted-girls/)**

## What's in this story

- A panoramic timeline of South Korea's sex ratio at birth (1980–2024) with key legal milestones, built with **ai2html**
- Line charts showing how ultrasound technology and zodiac superstitions (tiger, dragon, horse years) drove spikes in sex-selective abortion, built with **Datawrapper**
- Regional breakdowns revealing how neo-Confucian strongholds in Gyeongsang Province led the country in son preference
- A scrollytelling tile visualization where each tile represents 500 aborted girls, accumulating year by year into a final count of 408,043

## Data sources

- **[KOSIS (Korean Statistical Information Service)](https://kosis.kr/)** — annual birth counts by sex (1990–2024)
- **J Health Info Stat (2021), Table 3** — birth counts by sex (1981–1989)
- Academic papers on sex-selective abortion and son preference in South Korea (see project files)
